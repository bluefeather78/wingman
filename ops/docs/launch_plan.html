01

The honest starting line

Product update · 2026-09-07 · reconciled to the code

The monetization model changed: two-tier freemium, no trial

This plan was drafted around a 7-day free trial → single $9.99/mo plan → lockout on expiry. The product now ships a two-tier freemium model (TWO_TIER_AI_PLAN.md): every signed-in account has full app access, and the only thing that varies is the AI tier — a metered Free tier (a daily AI-action allowance) versus Unlimited at $4.99/mo. There is no trial and no app-access lockout — a lapsing or free account simply becomes a normal metered Free user. (The free-tier AI meter currently ships in observe-mode, not yet enforced.) Prices below are corrected to $4.99; the trial-era tactics — the trial-ending email, the "trial → paid" funnel — are kept as written and should be re-read as free → paid. A fuller freemium rewrite of the funnel and lifecycle messaging is a strategic call still to make.

A go-to-market plan is worthless if it front-runs the product's readiness. Three things must be true before a single paid dollar of acquisition is spent — two are blockers.

Blocker · money

Payments aren't wired

Stripe keys are absent, so the Upgrade button can't take money — the $4.99 Unlimited tier can't be sold at all. A lapsing account is no longer locked out (it drops to the metered Free tier), so the blocker is monetization, not access: nothing here earns a dollar until STRIPE_API_KEY / STRIPE_PRICE_ID are set and one real upgrade goes through checkout end-to-end.

Blocker · retention

Lifecycle email is dormant

Welcome, trial-ending and goodbye emails exist but the daily sweep is disarmed and the sending domain isn't verified. For a deadline product, the reminder is the retention loop. Arm the cron, verify DKIM/SPF, send one real welcome.

Gate · distribution

Minors change everything

The audience is largely under 18. App-store review, parental-consent flow, COPPA/FERPA posture and a "we don't sell a list of kids" privacy stance are launch prerequisites, not fine print. They also shape which channels are even allowed.

02

Two people, one purchase

The user and the buyer are different humans with different anxieties. Every message, screen and email has to satisfy both — the teen has to want it, the parent has to approve the card.

The user · grades 9–12

Wants to look impressive without the grind

  • Doesn't know what programs exist beyond the three their friends mention
  • Misses deadlines because they live in fifteen browser tabs
  • Responds to game-shaped progress — Quest Log, Fresh Finds, Home Base
  • Has no credit card
The buyer · parent / guardian

Wants a real return on $4.99

  • Anchors price against a single summer program that costs thousands
  • Needs to trust where their kid's data goes
  • Approves the trial, gets the trial-ending email, taps upgrade
  • Is reachable through parent Facebook groups and school newsletters

The pricing story writes itself: one missed fee-waiver deadline or one program you never heard of costs more than a year of Wingman. Lead with that math on the paywall and in the trial-ending email.

03

Positioning & the beachhead

Don't launch to "all high schoolers." Win one tight, deadline-driven, self-selecting segment first, then widen.

Positioning

The co-pilot for your extracurricular life

Not another college-admissions consultant, not a static list. Wingman finds opportunities matched to a student's actual profile, tracks every deadline, and hands them the exact next step for each application — verified against the program's own page, never invented. Category: opportunity discovery + deadline tracking, priced like an app, not a consultant.

Beachhead segment

Ambitious 10th & 11th graders, STEM-leaning

They apply to competitive summer programs and research competitions with hard fall/winter deadlines, they cluster in findable communities, and they feel the pain acutely. Land them, earn word-of-mouth and testimonials, then expand to arts/humanities and to 9th graders planning ahead.

05

SEO strategy — the catalog is the moat

Students search program names constantly, and those searches are low-competition and high-intent. A 1,200-row catalog that grows itself is 1,200 pages that can rank — this is the cheapest durable acquisition channel Wingman owns. Four layers:

Layer 1 · Programmatic pages

One indexable page per opportunity

Template a public page for every catalog row: name, org, eligibility, verified deadline, and the application checklist. Title pattern "[Program] — Deadlines, Eligibility & How to Apply (2027)". 1,200+ pages at launch, and every row the agents add ships a new one automatically.

Layer 2 · Hub / cluster pages

Rank for the category, funnel to the program

Curated hubs — "Summer research programs for high schoolers," "Free STEM summer programs," "[State] pre-college programs" — that internally link down to the program pages. Hubs win the fat head keywords; programs win the long tail. Classic topic-cluster structure.

Layer 3 · Question & comparison pages

Own the queries students actually type

"Is [program] worth it?", "[program] acceptance rate", "[program] vs [program]", "[program] deadline 2027". These are the exact phrasings in Reddit threads and Google autocomplete — high intent, almost no competition, and a natural place to show the tracker as the answer.

Layer 4 · Technical foundation

Make it crawlable and trustworthy

Ship robots.txt and a catalog-generated sitemap.xml; add Event/schema.org structured data with startDate/deadline so programs can win rich results; set canonical URLs; and server-render the SEO pages — a client-only Expo bundle indexes poorly, and these pages must be static HTML the crawler reads on first byte.

Freshness is a built-in advantage. The deadline agents update dates continuously, so program pages stay current — a signal static competitor lists can't match. Regenerate the sitemap on each catalog refresh so Google re-crawls what changed.

06

Advertising strategy — aim at the wallet

The hard constraint shapes everything: ad platforms forbid personalized targeting of under-18 users. So paid ads don't target teens directly — they target the buyer (parents), the gatekeeper (counselors), and use contextual rather than behavioral placement. Spend nothing until Phase 0 is done and the funnel converts; then start small and buy on payback, not vanity reach.

Channel

Google Search — high intent

Bid on parent-side, purchase-intent queries: "summer program for high schoolers," "college application help for my child." Search catches demand that already exists — best first dollar. Let SEO own the program-name long tail; buy the commercial head terms.

Channel

Meta — target parents, not kids

Facebook/Instagram to adults 35–55 with college-prep and parenting interests. This is where the "$4.99 vs. one program that costs thousands" creative lands. Compliant because the audience is adults.

Channel

Creator / short-form — contextual

Sponsor admissions-niche TikTok/YouTube creators rather than running personalized ads to teen accounts. The creator's audience does the targeting; the content is the ad. Highest early ROI for reaching students themselves.

Creative & message

Two ads, two audiences

Parent creative: the value math and the trust/privacy stance. Student-facing creator content: discovery FOMO ("programs nobody tells you about") and deadline urgency. Every ad routes to a "start your free trial" or "send this to a parent" flow — never a dead end for a user with no card.

Budget & discipline

Buy payback, spend seasonally

Concentrate spend in the Nov–Feb deadline peak when intent is highest. Cap early tests at a few hundred dollars per channel, measure cost per activated trial against the $4.99 LTV, and only scale the channel with the shortest payback. Kill anything that can't beat organic.

07

The launch, in four phases

Sequenced against the application calendar. Today is early September — Phases 0 and 1 have to finish before deadline season peaks.

Phase 0
Sep 2026 · ~3 wks
Goal: one dollar can be charged, one email can be sent.

Make it launchable

  • Wire Stripe and put a real trial through checkout to conversion.
  • Verify the sending domain (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) and arm the lifecycle sweep; test welcome + trial-ending against a real inbox.
  • Ship the app-store builds and pass review with the parental-consent flow.
  • Freeze the catalog quality bar — dead links and bad deadlines are the fastest way to lose a first-time user.
Phase 1
Oct 2026 · ~4 wks
Goal: proof the loop works on strangers, not friends.

Closed beta — 50–100 real students

  • Recruit from one channel (a counselor cohort or a single subject community). Hand out BETAUSER grants.
  • Watch the activation funnel you already instrument: signup → meaningful profile → first tracked program → came back.
  • Interview the students who didn't come back. Fix the drop-off before scaling it.
  • Collect 5–10 quotable testimonials and 3 before/after stories for launch.
Phase 2
Nov–Dec 2026
Goal: ride peak deadline season into paid conversions.

Seasonal public launch

  • Open signups. Turn on programmatic SEO pages and the Reddit + short-form push together.
  • Lean into urgency: "summer program deadlines are closing" is true and it's the exact moment the product is most valuable.
  • Launch a referral loop — a student who invites a friend extends their access; both win.
  • Measure free → paid conversion against activated free users (profile built, something tracked), never against all signups. (Under freemium there is no trial to "end" — the conversion trigger is hitting the free AI ceiling, once that meter is enforced.)
Phase 3
Jan 2027 →
Goal: turn a seasonal spike into a year-round habit.

Widen & retain

  • Expand beyond STEM to arts/humanities segments and to 9th-graders planning early.
  • Formalize the counselor/school channel — a free tier that makes Wingman the school's default tool.
  • Use deadline alerts + calendar sync as the always-on reason to keep the app installed between application seasons.
  • Double down on whichever Phase-2 channel produced the cheapest activated user.
08

Three loops that compound

Campaigns spend money once. Loops pay forward. These three are already latent in the product.

Content loop

Catalog → search → signup

Every opportunity the agents add becomes an indexable page that ranks for its own name and feeds new students back into the app. The catalog grows on its own; the SEO surface grows with it.

Habit loop

Deadline → alert → return

Tracked deadlines fire reminders and calendar events, which pull the student back exactly when a competitor's static list has gone cold. The reminder is the retention engine — which is why lifecycle email is a Phase-0 blocker.

Referral loop

Student → friend → both extend

Extracurriculars are social and competitive — students compare notes constantly. A grant-based referral (extend both accounts) rides that behavior instead of fighting it, and costs nothing but access.

10

Risks & guardrails

The ones most likely to bite, and the specific move that defuses each.

Payment friction (teens have no card)
The buyer isn't the user. Make the parent hand-off explicit — a "send this to a parent" flow and a trial-ending email addressed to the person with the card, not the kid.
Minors & privacy / COPPA-FERPA
Keep the "no marketing list, no data resale" stance as a selling point, not a liability. It's what earns counselor and parent trust — the two highest-value channels. Self-serve data export and account deletion are already built (/api/account/export, /api/account/delete behind a re-auth confirm) — point to them as proof, not promises.
Seasonality — spike then silence
A deadline app is loudest in fall/winter. Deadline alerts and calendar sync are the off-season reason to stay installed; build the year-round habit in Phase 3, not the spike.
Email deliverability to school inboxes
School Google Workspace MXes are unforgiving. Verify the domain, warm it slowly, ship a text/plain part, and let students edit the address off a locked school account.
Catalog trust — one bad link burns a first impression
A dead link or an invented deadline on day one loses the user for good. The link-health and page-verified-task guardrails already exist; keep the quality bar green before each push.
